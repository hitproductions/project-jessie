Jessie as a Slack app

Today Jessie is a chatbot: every booking is a typed back-and-forth. After the pivot, Jessie answers with buttons and forms. Typing still works for the first message and for questions. Every check Jessie runs today stays. What changes is how the details come in.

2 · Nothing is lost

What goes, what shrinks, what stays

Goes

  • Questions asked one message at a time
  • The confirmation card and typing "yes"
  • Reading dates, times, rooms and names from sentences, after the first message
  • Details carrying over from one request to the next
  • "Which one?" for bookings with the same title
  • The AI writing booking lists
  • Typing "reset"

Shrinks

  • The AI's job. From running the whole booking to understanding the first message and answering questions
  • The reply clean-up. Only typed replies still need it

Stays exactly

  • No double-booking
  • The room suits the session, and the usual rooms come first
  • Staff names and title initials from Bookers
  • Clients checked, new ones sent to the review sheet
  • The room's answer read back after booking
  • Only your own bookings can be changed
  • Priority and shared-booth approvals
  • Notices and logs
  • Calendar and Airtable as the source of truth
3 · The Slack screens

What people will see

Mockups with sample data. They use standard Slack parts: buttons, menus, date and time pickers, people pickers and forms.

Fields follow the choices. An internal room hides session type, client and engineer and asks for a title. A music session adds the arranger. Ticking Repeats adds the days and number of weeks.

Any number of clients. The client field searches Airtable as you type, so it isn't limited to Slack's 100 options.

Engineer defaults to you. Every requester is treated as a booker. Change it only when someone else is engineering.

Free times, not a guess. Like the "Available times" list in Slack's Google Calendar app: pick a room and date, and the time list shows only when it's free. A custom time that's taken is marked on the form, with the nearest free times.

4 · Review notes

Points raised, and how the shape handles them

Client list over 100Slack lists stop at 100 options
The client field searches Airtable as you type and shows the matches, so the client list can be any size. "No client" and new names are options too.
Engineer fieldAssume every requester is a booker
The engineer is set to the requester, and is changed only when someone else is engineering. It uses Slack's people picker, matched to Bookers by Slack user ID.
"Book a schedule"Not every booking is a session
The menu, buttons and form say "Book a schedule". Meetings and booths use the same form.
Buttons in the DMLike the PO Manager menu
Yes. "What would you like to do?" with buttons is the new front door (section 3).
5 · To decide

Open questions on the shape

  1. Front door. Should the menu appear in the DM on "hi", on a Home tab, or both?
  2. Review step. Should Book go straight through, or show a short review first (heads-ups, the dates of a repeat)?
  3. Typed changes. Should "make it 3pm" and "cancel my friday booking" keep working, or is the move to buttons complete?
  4. Availability. Should it be a form, typed questions, or both?

Based on the wide test of 13 Oct 2026 (8 of 20 scenarios passed; most failures came from details carrying over between requests and from free text not being read). Mockups use sample data.
