Jessie books studios, booths and meeting rooms from Slack. This deck compares how each part works today, by chat, with how it works as a Slack app with buttons and forms - what we keep, gain, lose and change.

October 2026
Where Jessie is today

A chat assistant with strict checks behind it

People type to Jessie in a Slack DM. An AI model reads the conversation; every booking then passes through checks written in code, against Airtable and the studio calendar. Live, used for testing only - no real bookings by staff yet.

Book

  • Studios, M booths, conference rooms, the Lobby
  • Recurring series
  • On behalf of a colleague
  • Taken room → nearest free times, other studios, another day

Change and find out

  • Cancel and move, with ownership checks and coordinator rights
  • "What's free…", "what do I have…"
  • Room facts from Airtable (formats, vocals in-room)

Approvals and notices

  • Priority requests: a higher-priority session asks a booked one to move
  • Shared M booths with a standing hold
  • Notices to the booker and engineer when a booking changes
Why change

The chat is where it breaks, not the checks

A wide test on 13 Oct: 20 scenarios, ~45 messages in everyday phrasing - Taglish, typos, shorthand, vague requests, two requests at once, changes of mind.

8 / 20 passed

12 failed or were partial. What held was everything code decides once the details are right: confirmation, taken rooms, refusing to double-book, cancel and move, series.

Over the last ten builds, fixes that moved a job into code held. Fixes to the AI's wording went in circles, one phrasing at a time.

6

Details carrying over between requests. An earlier "vo rec" looped a later question; a declined request's project and time were put on a new one; "what's free tomorrow afternoon?" was answered for 10-11 AM. Structural: the line between one request and the next is drawn by reading the thread.

4

Free text not read. "bukas", "stdio 7" (became Studio F), "9am for 2 hours", two bookings in one message (one dropped). Each can be patched; the space of phrasings is open-ended.

3

The AI's own wording. Its working notes leaked into replies; questions phrased its own way slipped past the code-written ones. Wording is the part of an AI that is hardest to pin down.

The new approach

Type to start. Tap to book. Code writes every fact.

Now · chat
1 · AskType the request
2 · Back and forthJessie asks for what's missing, one message at a time; the AI reads every reply in context
3 · CardA summary card; type "yes"
4 · Checks + calendarCode checks and books
After · Slack app
1 · AskType it, or tap Book a room in the menu
2 · FormOne form, pre-filled from what was typed; date and time pickers, rooms that are free, lists from Airtable
3 · BookPressing Book is the confirmation
4 · Checks + calendarThe same code checks and books; the result comes back with Move and Cancel

The AI keeps two jobs: understanding the first message (to pre-fill a form or find a booking) and answering questions.

Everything else is code and buttons: booking, cancelling, moving, approving. Each form stands alone, so nothing carries over from an earlier request.

Nothing is lost

What stays exactly as it is

The forms call the same back-end workflows the chat calls today. Every check that protects a booking runs on every form submission and every button press.

Booking checks

  • No double booking
  • The room suits the session; the usual rooms first (Airtable ranking)
  • Staff names and initials from Bookers
  • Clients checked; new ones sent for review
  • The room's own answer read back
  • Unusual length and past-date heads-ups

Changes and approvals

  • Only your own bookings, or your department's if you are a coordinator
  • A move creates the new booking before removing the old one
  • Priority and shared-booth approvals (one Google Sheet row per request, swept every 10 minutes)

Records and data

  • Calendar and Airtable remain the source of truth
  • The booking log, new-clients sheet and consent sheet
  • Notices to booker and engineer
  • Room facts: formats, vocals in-room, pairings

5 · Approvals, notices and reminders

Approve with a button; reminders free up unused rooms

Now · chat

The approval engine is built (one Google Sheet row per request, a sweep every 10 minutes, a finalize that cannot double-book). The way people answer is the weak part:

  • The holder types "yes" or "no"; the AI's router reads it.
  • The requester types "cleared with <name>" - this has looped.
  • Names have come out blank in the request messages.
  • The same request could open twice.
  • Only the booker is asked, not the engineer.
  • No reminders; notices only when someone else changes your booking.
After · Slack app Block Kit

The holder (and the engineer): agree and move, or decline.

The requester can withdraw; a held booth is two buttons.

Notices and reminders: engineer notice; studio sessions the day before; booths and meeting rooms every Monday 9 AM.
